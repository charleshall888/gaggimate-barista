# Milk & Drink Preparation

Milk science, steaming technique, and espresso drink recipes for home baristas.

---

## Milk Science & Steaming

### The Chemistry of Milk Foam

Milk foam is a three-way interaction between fat, protein, and air. Fat provides creaminess and mouthfeel. Proteins (casein and whey) act as surfactants — they stabilize air bubbles by forming elastic films around them. Sugars (lactose) contribute sweetness that becomes more perceptible as milk is heated.

**Milk Composition by Type**

| Milk | Fat (%) | Protein (%) | Sweetness | Foam Quality |
|------|---------|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| Whole (3.25%) | 3.25 | 3.4 | Medium | Excellent — creamy, stable, silky |
| 2% (Reduced fat) | 2.0 | 3.4 | Medium | Good — lighter body, still stable |
| Skim | 0.1 | 3.5 | Low | Stiff — high protein makes dry foam |
| Oat (Barista edition) | 3.0 | 1.0 | High | Very good — beta-glucans stabilize foam |
| Soy (Barista edition) | 2.0 | 3.5 | Low | Good — high protein, can curdle with acid |
| Almond | 1.5 | 0.5 | Low | Poor — thin, unstable, separates quickly |
| Coconut | 2.0 | 0.2 | Medium | Fair — creamy but foam collapses fast |

**Temperature Effects on Milk**

| Temperature | What Happens | Flavor Effect | Texture Effect |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| < 50°C (122°F) | Proteins intact, fats liquid | Mild, raw taste | Thin, won't hold foam well |
| 50-60°C (122-140°F) | Whey proteins begin unfolding | Sweetness emerging | Foam starts stabilizing |
| 60-65°C (140-149°F) | Protein denaturation begins; lactose perception increases | Sweet, clean | Ideal microfoam window opens |
| 65-70°C (149-158°F) | Peak lactose sweetness; proteins fully denatured | Maximum natural sweetness | Best microfoam — silky, glossy |
| > 70°C (158°F+) | Maillard browning begins; proteins aggregate | Scalded, flat, bitter | Foam breaks down, becomes bubbly |

**Key thresholds to remember:**
- **60°C** — Protein denaturation begins. This is when foam starts getting good.
- **65-70°C** — The sweet spot. Lactose is most perceptible, microfoam is most stable.
- **70°C** — The danger line. Above here, quality degrades rapidly.

### Microfoam vs Macrofoam

The difference between a velvety flat white and a dry cappuccino comes down to foam structure.

| Property | Microfoam | Macrofoam |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Bubble size | < 0.5mm (invisible) | 1-3mm (visible) |
| Visual | Glossy, wet paint sheen | Matte, puffy, cloud-like |
| Texture | Silky, velvety, liquid | Airy, dry, meringue-like |
| Pour behavior | Flows like cream, holds patterns | Sits on top, resists mixing |
| Best for | Flat whites, cortados, latte art | Traditional cappuccinos, macchiatos |

Modern specialty coffee overwhelmingly favors microfoam — it integrates with espresso rather than sitting on top. Traditional Italian style uses more macrofoam for texture contrast. Neither is "wrong," but microfoam gives you more versatility and better latte art potential.

### Steaming Technique

Steaming has three distinct phases. Each has a different wand position, sound, and goal.

**Steaming Phases**

| Phase | Goal | Wand Position | Duration | Sound |
|-------|------|--------------|----------|-------|
| **Stretching** | Introduce air | Tip just below surface (~5mm) | 3-5 sec | Gentle "tsss" — paper tearing |
| **Rolling** | Create vortex, break bubbles | Tip submerged ~1cm, angled | Until 55°C | Quiet hum — no hissing |
| **Polishing** | Integrate texture | Same position, let heat finish | Until 65°C | Near-silent |

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Start cold.** Always begin with cold milk (ideally 4°C / 39°F). Cold milk gives you more time in the stretching phase before hitting temperature thresholds.
2. **Purge the wand.** Clear condensation before inserting into milk.
3. **Stretch (3-5 seconds).** Position the steam tip just below the surface. You should hear a gentle "tsss" — rhythmic chirping. Each chirp introduces a tiny amount of air. For microfoam drinks (flat white, cortado), stretch less (2-3 seconds). For cappuccino, stretch more (4-6 seconds).
4. **Submerge and roll.** Drop the tip ~1cm below the surface and angle the pitcher to create a vortex. This spins the milk, breaking large bubbles into micro-bubbles. The sound should be a quiet hum — if you hear hissing, the tip is too high.
5. **Polish to temperature.** Hold position and let the vortex polish the texture until you reach 65°C. The pitcher should feel "too hot to hold comfortably" — that's roughly 65°C.
6. **Stop and swirl.** Turn off steam, remove wand, purge immediately. Give the pitcher a firm tap on the counter (pops surface bubbles) and swirl to maintain the glossy texture.

**Common Steaming Problems**

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Large, visible bubbles | Too much air / tip too high | Reduce stretching time; keep tip just below surface |
| Scalded / burnt taste | Milk over 70°C | Stop earlier; use a thermometer until you calibrate by touch |
| Screaming / screeching | Tip too deep + full steam pressure | Raise tip slightly; ensure vortex is established |
| Foam too stiff / dry | Over-stretched, too much air introduced | Shorten stretching phase; more rolling time |
| No foam at all | Tip too deep during stretch phase | Raise tip until you hear the "tsss" |
| Foam separates quickly | Low-protein milk or overheated | Switch to barista-edition alt-milk; don't exceed 65°C |

### Milk Types & Alternatives

**Performance Matrix**

| Milk Type | Fat (%) | Protein (%) | Sweetness | Foam Quality | Latte Art | Notes |
|-----------|---------|-------------|-----------|--------------|-----------|-------|
| Whole dairy | 3.25 | 3.4 | Medium | Excellent | Excellent | The gold standard |
| 2% dairy | 2.0 | 3.4 | Medium | Very good | Very good | Slightly less body |
| Skim dairy | 0.1 | 3.5 | Low | Stiff/dry | Fair | Foam is stable but unpleasant texture |
| Oat (Barista) | 3.0 | 1.0 | High | Very good | Good | Best dairy alternative overall |
| Soy (Barista) | 2.0 | 3.5 | Low | Good | Fair | High protein but acid-sensitive |
| Almond | 1.5 | 0.5 | Low | Poor | Poor | Low protein = unstable foam |
| Coconut | 2.0 | 0.2 | Medium | Fair | Poor | Creamy body but foam collapses |

**Why oat milk works:** Barista-edition oat milks contain added fats (usually rapeseed/canola oil) and beta-glucans — soluble fibers from the oats that mimic the stabilizing role of dairy proteins. This gives oat milk unusually good foam for a plant-based option.

**The curdling problem with plant milks:**

Plant milks curdle when two conditions combine:
1. **Acidity** — Espresso pH ranges from 4.5-6.0. Light roasts are more acidic.
2. **Heat** — Above 70°C, proteins become more vulnerable to acid-induced coagulation.

Prevention strategies:
- Use barista-edition milks (formulated with stabilizers and added calcium)
- Steam to 60-65°C, not higher — lower temperature reduces curdling risk
- Pour milk into espresso (not espresso into milk) — gradual pH change is gentler
- Choose medium roasts over very light roasts for plant-milk drinks (lower acidity)
- Temper the milk: add a small splash of cold milk to the espresso first, then pour the steamed milk

### Gaggia Classic Pro Single Boiler Workflow

Single boiler machines like the Gaggia Classic Pro can't brew and steam simultaneously. You need to transition between brew and steam modes, which means managing timing and temperature.

**Workflow Options**

| Step | Shot First (Recommended) | Steam First |
|------|-------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Pull espresso shot | Steam milk, set aside |
| 2 | Switch to steam mode | Purge boiler, switch to brew mode |
| 3 | Wait for steam ready (~30s) | Wait for brew temp to stabilize |
| 4 | Purge, then steam milk | Pull espresso shot |
| 5 | Purge wand, combine | Combine (milk may have degraded) |
| **Pros** | Fresh steamed milk; better texture | Espresso is freshest possible |
| **Cons** | Espresso sits ~60s while you steam | Steamed milk degrades while waiting |
| **Best for** | Most drinks (milk quality matters more) | Straight espresso with a splash |

**Recommended sequence:** Shot first. Espresso degrades more gracefully than steamed milk — a 60-second-old shot still tastes good, but microfoam starts separating after 30 seconds. With Gaggimate, the machine transitions to steam mode quickly (~20-30s), minimizing the gap.

**Tip:** Start heating your milk pitcher (fill with hot water, dump before use) so cold milk doesn't steal heat from your steaming process.

### Latte Art Basics

Latte art requires two things: properly steamed microfoam and controlled pouring technique.

**Core Patterns**

| Pattern | Difficulty | Technique Summary |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Heart | Beginner | Pour center, push through at the end |
| Tulip | Intermediate | Sequential pours with push-through layers |
| Rosetta | Advanced | Side-to-side wiggle while pulling back, push through |

**Fundamental principles:**
- **High to mix, low to draw.** Start your pour from ~3cm above the cup to mix milk and espresso (creates the brown canvas). Drop the spout close to the surface (~1cm) when you want white foam to appear on top.
- **Flow rate matters.** Slow, steady flow for mixing; slightly faster flow for drawing patterns.
- **Cup angle helps.** Tilt the cup ~20° toward you while pouring, then level it as the cup fills.
- **Speed over precision.** Latte art must happen while the foam is fresh. A quick, confident pour with imperfect art beats a slow, hesitant one where the foam has separated.

## Espresso Drinks

### Coffee First, Drink Second

Extract for the bean's best expression, then match the right drink format. Never adjust grind, ratio, pressure, or temperature to "make the shot work in milk." If the extraction tastes great as espresso, the right milk drink will showcase it. If the user wants more concentration for a specific drink, suggest a smaller drink format rather than compromising extraction.

**Drink Format Recommendations**

| Shot Character | Recommended Format | Milk Volume | Why |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------|-----|
| Bright, fruity, delicate | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Small milk volume preserves acidity and fruit |
| Sweet, balanced, medium body | Cappuccino or flat white | 4-6 oz | Enough milk to complement without drowning |
| Intense, heavy body, bold | Latte (if desired) | 8-10 oz | More milk balances intensity |
| Clarity-focused (turbo/allonge) | Cortado or piccolo | 2-3 oz | Lighter body gets lost in large drinks |

### Classic Milk Drinks

**Drink Specifications**

| Drink | Espresso | Milk | Foam | Total Volume | Espresso:Milk | Vessel |
|-------|----------|------|------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| Macchiato | 1 oz (single) | 0.5 oz | Dollop of foam | ~2 oz | 1:0.5 | 2-3 oz demitasse |
| Cortado | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | Thin microfoam | ~4 oz | 1:1 | 4 oz glass (Gibraltar) |
| Piccolo | 1 oz (single ristretto) | 2-3 oz | Thin microfoam | ~3.5 oz | 1:2.5 | 3-4 oz glass |
| Cappuccino (traditional) | 2 oz (double) | 2 oz | 2 oz stiff foam | ~6 oz | 1:1:1 | 5-6 oz ceramic |
| Cappuccino (modern) | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Microfoam throughout | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 6 oz ceramic |
| Flat white | 2 oz (double) | 4 oz | Very thin microfoam | ~6 oz | 1:2 | 5-6 oz ceramic |
| Latte | 2 oz (double) | 8-10 oz | Thin foam layer | ~10-12 oz | 1:4-5 | 8-12 oz glass or ceramic |

**Macchiato** — "Stained" in Italian. A single or double espresso with a small dollop of foam on top. The milk is a garnish, not a partner. This is about tasting the espresso with just a touch of sweetness and texture. Best for: bold, dark-roasted espresso you want to appreciate with a slight softening.

**Cortado** — Equal parts espresso and steamed milk. The milk tames acidity and adds body without masking the coffee's character. The 1:1 ratio means the espresso is still clearly the star. Traditionally served in a 4 oz Gibraltar glass. Best for: bright, fruity shots where you want balance without losing origin character.

**Piccolo** — A single ristretto shot topped with steamed milk in a small glass. More milk-forward than a macchiato but smaller and more concentrated than a cappuccino. Best for: light roasts with delicate flavors that shine through a small milk volume.

**Cappuccino (Traditional)** — The classic Italian formula: equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and foam (1:1:1). The thick foam cap creates texture contrast with the liquid underneath. More about mouthfeel than latte art. Best for: medium roasts with chocolate and nut notes.

**Cappuccino (Modern Specialty)** — Uses microfoam throughout rather than a distinct foam layer. The result is more integrated — silky from top to bottom. More milk than a cortado, less than a latte. This is the dominant style in specialty coffee today. Best for: versatile; works with most roast levels and flavor profiles.

**Flat White** — Very similar to a modern cappuccino but with an even thinner microfoam layer. The emphasis is on velvety texture and strong coffee flavor. Originated in Australia/New Zealand. Some cafes use a ristretto double shot for more intensity. Best for: medium-bodied shots where you want maximum coffee-to-milk integration.

**Latte** — The most milk-forward espresso drink. The large milk volume makes it mild and approachable, but also the easiest drink in which to lose the coffee's character. Best for: intense, heavy-bodied shots that can stand up to dilution, or when you simply want a gentle, milky coffee.

### Iced & Alternative Drinks

**Iced Drink Specifications**

| Drink | Method | Espresso | Other | Total Volume | Key Technique |
|-------|--------|----------|-------|-------------|---------------|
| Japanese iced | Flash brew over ice | Double (2 oz) | 4-6 oz ice | ~6-8 oz | Brew directly onto ice; preserves aromatics |
| Shakerato | Shaken with ice | Double (2 oz) | Ice + optional sugar | ~4 oz | Shake 15s in cocktail shaker; strain into glass |
| Espresso tonic | Layered | Double (2 oz) | 4-6 oz tonic water + ice | ~8 oz | Pour espresso slowly over tonic; preserves layers |
| Affogato | Pour-over dessert | Single or double | 1 scoop gelato/ice cream | ~4-6 oz | Pour hot espresso over frozen gelato |

**Japanese Iced Espresso** — Pull a regular double shot directly over 100-150g of ice in the cup. The rapid cooling locks in volatile aromatics that dissipate when espresso cools slowly. The result is a brighter, more aromatic iced coffee than simply cooling a shot. Use the same profile and grind as your hot espresso — no adjustments needed.

**Shakerato** — Pull a double shot into a cocktail shaker with ice (and optionally a barspoon of simple syrup or demerara sugar). Shake vigorously for 10-15 seconds and strain into a chilled coupe or nick & nora glass. The shaking creates a light, frothy crema-like layer. Elegant and refreshing.

**Espresso Tonic** — Fill a glass with ice and 4-6 oz of quality tonic water. Slowly pour a double espresso over the back of a spoon to float it on top. The layered presentation is visually striking. Stir before drinking. The quinine bitterness in tonic complements bright, fruity espresso beautifully — avoid this with dark roasts, which clash with the tonic's bitterness.

**Affogato** — More dessert than drink. Place a scoop of good vanilla gelato (or ice cream) in a small bowl or glass. Pour a freshly pulled shot over it. Eat/drink immediately as the hot espresso melts into the cold gelato. Best with medium to dark roasts that have chocolate, caramel, or nutty notes.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Oat milk curdling | Acidity + heat > 70°C | Steam cooler (60-65°C); pour milk into espresso; use barista edition |
| Scalded / burnt taste | Milk overheated past 70°C | Stop at 65°C; use a thermometer until calibrated by touch |
| No latte art contrast | Milk too thin or too thick | Adjust stretching time; ensure glossy microfoam texture |
| Foam too thick / dry | Over-stretched (too much air) | Reduce stretching phase to 2-3 seconds for microfoam drinks |
| Coffee flavor lost in drink | Too much milk for the shot | Move to a smaller format: latte → cappuccino → cortado |
| Foam separates quickly | Overheated or low-protein milk | Steam to 65°C max; use whole dairy or barista-edition alt-milk |
| Espresso and milk don't integrate | Poured too slowly or from too high | Pour faster; bring spout close to surface earlier |
| Drink lukewarm | Cold cup absorbing heat | Pre-heat cups with hot water; steam milk slightly hotter (68°C) |

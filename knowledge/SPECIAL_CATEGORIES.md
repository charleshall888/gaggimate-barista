# Special Categories: Decaf & Blends

Guidance for coffees that don't fit the single-origin specialty mold — decaffeinated beans and espresso blends. Both require adjusted extraction strategies.

---

## Decaf Espresso

### How Decaffeination Changes the Bean

All decaffeination methods remove caffeine from green (unroasted) beans by soaking them in a solvent — water, chemical, or supercritical CO2. This process fundamentally alters the bean's physical structure:

| Property | Regular Bean | Decaf Bean | Impact |
|----------|-------------|------------|--------|
| **Porosity** | 0.25–0.35 | 0.38–0.49 | Water penetrates faster → extracts faster |
| **Density** | Higher | Lower | First crack arrives earlier during roasting |
| **CO2 content** | Normal | Reduced | Less crema, less puck resistance |
| **Color** | Reflects roast level | Darker at same roast level | Easy to over-roast visually |
| **Aromatics** | Full spectrum | Reduced volatiles | Some flavor compounds lost with caffeine |
| **Cell structure** | Intact | More open, fractured | Oils migrate to surface faster → stales faster |

**Practical consequence:** Decaf beans extract more readily than their caffeinated counterparts. If you treat them identically, you'll over-extract — pulling bitter, hollow, astringent shots.

### Decaf Process Comparison

| Process | How It Works | Caffeine Removed | Flavor Impact | Chemical Use | Best For |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|-------------|----------|
| **Swiss Water (SWP)** | Green coffee extract (GCE) saturated with all solubles *except* caffeine draws caffeine out via osmosis | 99.9% | Clean, transparent; preserves structure and clarity | None — water only | Washed coffees; clean cup profiles |
| **Sugarcane EA** | Ethyl acetate derived from sugarcane fermentation selectively bonds to caffeine | ~97% | Retains brightness and fruit; can add subtle sweetness or "tropical" notes | EA (naturally occurring, food-safe) | Light/medium roasts; fruit-forward coffees |
| **Supercritical CO2** | CO2 at high pressure/temperature becomes a selective solvent for caffeine | 97–99% | Preserves most original character; mild, balanced | CO2 (food-grade, recycled) | Large-scale production; flavor preservation |
| **Mountain Water (MWP)** | Similar to Swiss Water but uses glacial water from Pico de Orizaba; common in Mexican decaf | 99.9% | Clean, balanced; slightly different mineral profile than SWP | None — water only | Central/South American coffees |

**Avoid:** Methylene chloride (MC) processed decafs. While the solvent burns off during roasting, MC is classified as a probable carcinogen. Most specialty roasters have moved away from it. If the process isn't stated, ask.

### Extraction Adjustments for Decaf

Decaf's higher porosity and lower density mean it needs a gentler approach across the board:

| Variable | Regular Espresso | Decaf Adjustment | Why |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----|
| **Grind** | Your baseline | 1–3 macro steps *coarser* | Porous structure offers less flow resistance |
| **Temperature** | Per roast level | -2 to -4°C from baseline | Extracts faster; lower temp avoids bitterness |
| **Pressure** | Per matrix | -1 to -2 bar | Less force needed; reduces harsh extraction |
| **Ratio** | Per style | Same or slightly longer (1:2.5) | Longer ratio develops sweetness in a less complex cup |
| **Pre-infusion** | Standard | Shorter (3–5 seconds) | Porous puck saturates faster |
| **Dose** | = basket size | Same (never underdose) | Porosity means the basket may look fuller — trust the scale |

**Expect:** Decaf shots flow *noticeably* faster at the same grind setting. Baristas report needing 5–7 clicks finer than expected on commercial grinders. On the Sette 270, start 2–3 macro steps coarser than your regular setting and adjust from there — the grind gap is real but varies by bean.

### Profile Recommendations

| Profile | Suitability | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| **Dark Roast Gentle** | Excellent | Low pressure (6–7 bar) + decline matches decaf's needs |
| **Classic 9-Bar** (modified) | Good | Reduce to 7–8 bar; shorten pre-infusion |
| **Bloom Profile** | Use with caution | Decaf has less CO2 — the bloom pause offers less benefit; shorten to 3–5 seconds |
| **Turbo** | Avoid | Decaf's porosity + coarse grind + low pressure = nearly zero resistance; shot will gush |

### Why Decaf Tastes "Flat" (And How to Fix It)

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Hollow, lifeless cup | Over-extraction from standard settings | Coarser grind, lower temp, lower pressure |
| No sweetness, all bitterness | Stale beans (decaf stales 2x faster) | Buy fresher; use within 1–2 weeks of roast |
| Cardboard/papery notes | Old beans or MC processing | Try SWP or EA process from a specialty roaster |
| Thin body, watery | Insufficient dose or channeling | Maintain full dose; improve puck prep |
| Bland, no character | Poor-quality green coffee decaffeinated | Seek specialty-grade decaf (EA or SWP from named farms) |

**The freshness rule:** Decaf should be treated as **one freshness tier more urgent** than the equivalent regular coffee. If a medium roast peaks at 10–21 days, aim to use decaf medium roast within 7–14 days. The open cell structure accelerates staling.

## Espresso Blends

### Why Blends Exist

Blends aren't inferior to single origins — they serve different goals:

- **Consistency:** Single origins change with each harvest. Blends can be reformulated to maintain a target flavor profile year-round.
- **Complementary flavors:** A roaster can combine a Brazilian base (body, sweetness, low acidity) with a Kenyan component (brightness, complexity) and an Ethiopian (floral, fruity top notes) to build something no single origin provides.
- **Cost management:** Premium components can be stretched with reliable, affordable base coffees without sacrificing overall quality.
- **Designed for espresso:** Many blends are roasted specifically for espresso extraction — optimized for the 25–30 second, high-pressure encounter in ways that some single origins aren't.
- **Milk drink optimization:** Blends are often developed to taste good *through* milk — more forgiving than delicate single origins in lattes and cappuccinos.

### The Mixed Roast Level Problem

Many blends combine components roasted to different levels — for example, a dark Brazilian base with a medium Ethiopian accent. This creates an extraction dilemma:

**The physics:** Different roast levels extract at different rates. At any given temperature, dark-roast particles are more soluble and extract faster than light-roast particles. A fixed set of parameters will simultaneously over-extract the dark component and under-extract the light one.

### Temperature Compromise Strategies

| Blend Composition | Temperature Strategy | Target | Why |
|-------------------|---------------------|--------|-----|
| Mostly dark + some medium | Target the dark: 89–91°C | Protect the majority from bitterness | Small medium component adds subtle brightness |
| Even dark/medium split | Split the difference: 91–92°C | Neither perfect, both acceptable | Best overall balance |
| Mostly medium + some light | Target the medium: 92–93°C | Medium is the backbone flavor | Light component adds complexity |
| Single roast level | Match to roast level directly | No compromise needed | Easier to dial in |

**Key insight:** Post-blend roasting (roasting all components together) eliminates this problem but limits the roaster's control. Pre-blend roasting (roasting each origin separately, then mixing) gives more flavor control but creates the mixed-extraction challenge. Most specialty blends are post-blended; most traditional Italian blends are too.

### Blend Archetypes

#### Italian Traditional
- **Character:** Dark chocolate, toasted nuts, low acidity, heavy body, lingering bittersweet finish
- **Roast:** Medium-dark to dark; often includes Robusta (5–20%) for crema and body
- **Approach:** 88–91°C, 8–9 bar, 1:1.5 to 1:2 ratio
- **Profile:** Classic 9-Bar or Lever Decline; pressure decline helps manage bitterness
- **Best as:** Straight espresso, cappuccino, latte — designed for milk

#### Australian
- **Character:** Balanced, medium body, mild fruit, caramel sweetness, approachable
- **Roast:** Medium; typically all Arabica, often from Brazil + Central America + one African origin
- **Approach:** 91–93°C, 9 bar, 1:2 ratio
- **Profile:** Classic 9-Bar; these blends are designed for the standard approach
- **Best as:** Flat white (the Australian national drink), long black, milk drinks

#### Nordic / Third Wave
- **Character:** Lighter, origin-forward, seasonal components, more acidity than traditional blends, complex
- **Roast:** Medium-light to medium; all specialty-grade Arabica
- **Approach:** 93–95°C, 8–9 bar, 1:2 to 1:2.5 ratio
- **Profile:** Bloom profile or gentle ramp — treats the blend more like a single origin
- **Best as:** Cortado, flat white, or straight; the lighter profile can get lost in large lattes

### Single Origin vs Blend: Quick Comparison

| Factor | Single Origin | Blend |
|--------|--------------|-------|
| **Flavor complexity** | Origin-specific; can be extraordinary or one-note | Engineered balance; multiple dimensions |
| **Consistency** | Changes with harvest/lot | Reformulated for consistency |
| **Seasonality** | Best at peak freshness from origin | Available year-round |
| **Dialing in** | Can be finicky (especially light/experimental) | Generally more forgiving |
| **Milk compatibility** | Varies — delicate SOs can disappear | Usually designed for milk |
| **Transparency** | Full traceability to farm/lot | Components may not be named |
| **Cost** | Varies widely ($15–$100+/bag) | Typically moderate ($14–$22/bag) |
| **Learning value** | Teaches you about origins and processing | Teaches you about extraction fundamentals |

### Extraction Tips for Blends

1. **Start with the roaster's recommendations.** Blends are designed for a specific approach — the roaster's suggested dose, ratio, and temp are usually well-tested.
2. **Default to 1:2 ratio at the roast-level temperature.** Blends are more forgiving of the classic approach than most single origins.
3. **If the blend has named origins, look them up.** Knowing the components helps you understand what to emphasize (e.g., if the blend is 70% Brazilian + 30% Ethiopian, lean toward the Brazilian extraction parameters).
4. **Blends age more gracefully.** The multiple components mean that as one fades, others may still be contributing. Don't rush through a bag of blend the way you would a delicate single origin.
5. **Pressure profile matters less.** Standard 9-bar flat profiles work well for most blends. Save the exotic pressure curves for single origins where you're chasing specific flavors.

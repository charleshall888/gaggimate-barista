# Coffee Research Checklist

Detailed procedures for researching coffee beans.

---

## Web Search Strategy

### Primary Sources (in order of reliability)
1. **Roaster's website** — Most accurate for their own coffee
2. **Specialty coffee databases** — Trade Coffee, Bean Box listings
3. **Coffee review sites** — detailed tasting notes and roast analysis

### Search Queries
- `"[roaster name]" "[coffee name]"` — exact match
- `"[roaster name]" [origin] [process]` — if name unclear
- `[origin] [variety] espresso` — for general origin guidance

## Inferring Roast Level

When roast level isn't stated, infer from tasting notes:

### Light Roast Indicators
- Fruit-forward: blueberry, strawberry, citrus, stone fruit
- Floral: jasmine, lavender, bergamot
- Tea-like descriptors
- "Bright," "vibrant," "complex acidity"
- Wine-like or fermented notes (especially naturals)

### Medium Roast Indicators
- Balanced fruit + chocolate
- Nutty: almond, hazelnut
- Caramel, brown sugar
- "Balanced," "smooth," "approachable"
- Some origin character with sweetness

### Dark Roast Indicators
- Chocolate dominant: dark chocolate, cocoa
- Roasty: toasted, smoky
- Low acidity descriptors
- "Bold," "full-bodied," "rich"
- Lacking origin-specific fruit notes

## Origin Extraction Patterns

### Brazilian
- **Character:** Nutty, chocolate, low acidity, full body
- **Processing:** Natural or pulped natural common
- **Approach:** Lower temps (90-93°C), classic 9-bar or gentle profiles
- **Ratio:** 1:2 or shorter for intensity

### Central American (Guatemala, Honduras)
- **Character:** Balanced, chocolate, mild fruit, clean sweetness
- **Processing:** Mostly washed
- **Approach:** Classic profiles, 92-94°C, 9 bar
- **Ratio:** 1:2 standard

### Colombian
- **Character:** Balanced, sweet, mild acidity, caramel
- **Processing:** Mostly washed; growing experimental/anaerobic scene
- **Approach:** Classic 9-bar works well, 92-94°C
- **Ratio:** 1:2 standard

### Costa Rican
- **Character:** Clean sweetness, stone fruit, honey-forward when honey processed
- **Processing:** Known for honey process innovation (yellow → red → black, increasing sweetness and body); also excellent washed
- **Approach:** Washed: 92-94°C, classic 9-bar. Honey: 92-94°C, 8-9 bar, bloom profile for black honey
- **Ratio:** 1:2 for washed; 1:2 to 1:2.5 for honey (longer for black honey)

### Ethiopian
- **Character:** Fruit-forward, floral, complex
- **Processing:** Often natural (fruit bomb) or washed (floral, tea-like)
- **Approach:** Higher temps (94-96°C), bloom profiles for naturals
- **Ratio:** 1:2.5 for fruit development

### Indian
- **Character:** Earthy, spicy, heavy body (Monsooned Malabar); emerging specialty washed/natural scene with cleaner profiles
- **Processing:** Monsooned (unique — beans exposed to monsoon winds for weeks, reducing acidity and increasing body); washed and natural for specialty
- **Approach:** Monsooned: lower temps (89-91°C), 7-8 bar, shorter ratio. Specialty washed: 92-94°C standard
- **Ratio:** Monsooned: 1:1.5 to 1:2 for body. Specialty: 1:2 standard

### Indonesian (Sumatra, Java)
- **Character:** Earthy, herbal, full body, cedar, tobacco
- **Processing:** Wet-hulled (Giling Basah) — unique to Indonesia, creates the distinctive earthy character
- **Approach:** Lower temps (90-92°C), shorter ratios, 8-9 bar
- **Ratio:** 1:1.5 to 1:2 for body

### Kenyan
- **Character:** Bright, berry, blackcurrant, wine-like acidity, complex
- **Processing:** Washed (double fermentation — extended 24-72 hour soak develops signature acidity)
- **Approach:** High temps (94-96°C) to extract sweetness behind the acidity
- **Ratio:** 1:2.5 to 1:3 for clarity

### Mexican
- **Character:** Mild, chocolate, nutty, balanced acidity, approachable
- **Processing:** Mostly washed; large organic production sector
- **Approach:** Classic profiles, 92-94°C, 9 bar. Forgiving and consistent
- **Ratio:** 1:2 standard

### Panamanian (Gesha Focus)
- **Character:** Jasmine, bergamot, tropical fruit, tea-like body — among the most delicate and complex coffees in the world
- **Processing:** Washed (floral clarity), natural (fruit-forward), honey
- **Approach:** Gentle extraction: 93-95°C, 7-8 bar, bloom profile. Avoid high pressure — Gesha is fragile
- **Ratio:** 1:2.5 to 1:3 for clarity and aromatics

### Papua New Guinean
- **Character:** Herbal, tropical fruit, brown sugar, medium body, sometimes wild/rustic
- **Processing:** Washed (most common); some natural. Quality can be inconsistent due to infrastructure challenges
- **Approach:** 92-94°C, 8-9 bar, classic profiles. Treat like a medium-bodied Central American
- **Ratio:** 1:2 to 1:2.5

### Peruvian
- **Character:** Mild, sweet, chocolate, stone fruit, clean. Growing specialty reputation
- **Processing:** Washed dominant; significant organic and Fair Trade production
- **Approach:** Classic profiles, 92-94°C, 9 bar. Reliable and approachable
- **Ratio:** 1:2 standard

### Rwandan / Burundian
- **Character:** Bright, berry, citrus, floral — African character distinct from Ethiopian and Kenyan. Red fruit, cola, sometimes savory
- **Processing:** Washed (centralized washing stations ensure consistency)
- **Approach:** 93-95°C, 8-9 bar, bloom profile enhances fruit development
- **Ratio:** 1:2.5 for clarity
- **Note:** Occasional "potato defect" (musty/raw potato flavor) caused by antestia bug — not a preparation issue; discard affected beans

### Yemeni
- **Character:** Wild, fruity, wine-like, dried fruit, spice, chocolate — ancient and unique. Heirloom varieties with tremendous genetic diversity
- **Processing:** Natural (traditional dry process on rooftops); very high altitude (1500-2500+ masl)
- **Approach:** 93-95°C, 7-8 bar, bloom profile. Treat like an intense natural — lower pressure keeps the wildness in check
- **Ratio:** 1:2 to 1:2.5

## Variety Extraction Patterns

### Altitude and Density

Altitude affects bean density, which directly impacts extraction behavior. Denser beans are harder to extract and need more energy (finer grind, higher temperature, or longer time).

| Altitude (masl) | Density | Grind Approach | Temp Approach | Notes |
|-----------------|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| < 1200 | Low | Standard or slightly coarser | Per roast level | Easy to extract; watch for over-extraction |
| 1200–1600 | Medium | Standard baseline | Per roast level | Most coffees fall here |
| 1600–2000 | High | Standard or slightly finer | +1°C from roast baseline | Needs more extraction energy; rewards patience |
| 2000+ | Very high | Finer grind, longer pre-infusion | +1-2°C from roast baseline | Dense, complex; consider bloom profile |

**Important:** Altitude is a proxy for density, not a guarantee. Variety, processing, and roast level all modify density. A dark-roasted high-altitude bean will still be less dense than a light-roasted one from the same farm.

### Variety Profiles

#### Gesha / Geisha
- **Density:** Low to medium despite high altitude — open, elongated bean structure
- **Character:** Jasmine, bergamot, tropical fruit, tea-like body. The most aromatic and delicate variety
- **Approach:** Gentle — 93-95°C, 7-8 bar, bloom profile, 1:2.5-1:3 ratio. High pressure destroys the floral top notes
- **Risk:** Easy to over-extract into bitterness that masks the florals. If in doubt, pull longer and lighter

#### SL28 / SL34
- **Density:** High — developed by Scott Labs in Kenya for drought resistance; dense, hard beans
- **Character:** Complex acidity, blackcurrant, grapefruit, wine-like depth. The backbone of Kenyan coffee
- **Approach:** High temps (94-96°C) to unlock acidity; 8-9 bar; 1:2.5-1:3 for clarity
- **Risk:** Under-extraction leaves them harsh and sour. These beans need full extraction to show their sweetness

#### 74110 / Ethiopian Landraces
- **Density:** Medium to high (varies widely — wild genetic diversity)
- **Character:** Hugely variable — fruit, floral, spice, wine, chocolate. Each landrace is unique
- **Approach:** Start with 94°C, 8 bar, bloom profile, 1:2.2 ratio. Adjust based on specific character
- **Risk:** The diversity means there's no single "right" approach. Taste and adjust; what works for one 74110 lot may not work for the next

#### Bourbon
- **Density:** Medium — the classic balanced variety
- **Character:** Sweet, balanced, caramel, stone fruit, chocolate. Often described as the benchmark for quality
- **Approach:** 92-94°C, 9 bar, classic profiles work beautifully. Bourbon is forgiving
- **Risk:** Low — Bourbon is the all-rounder. If it's tasting flat, it's likely a freshness or roast issue, not a variety issue

#### Typica
- **Density:** Medium — the original Arabica variety from which most others descend
- **Character:** Clean, sweet, mild, transparent. Lets terroir and processing shine through rather than imposing its own character
- **Approach:** 92-94°C, 9 bar, classic profiles. Similar to Bourbon but slightly more delicate
- **Risk:** Can taste thin if under-extracted. Ensure adequate extraction yield

#### Caturra / Catuai
- **Density:** Medium-high — compact mutations of Bourbon (Caturra) and Bourbon × Mundo Novo cross (Catuai)
- **Character:** Bright acidity, citrus, clean sweetness. More lively than Bourbon, slightly less sweet
- **Approach:** 93-95°C, 9 bar, classic or bloom. Can handle slightly higher extraction energy than Bourbon
- **Risk:** Brightness can read as sourness if under-extracted. Don't be afraid of higher temps

#### Modern Hybrids (Castillo, Colombia, Marsellesa)
- **Density:** Medium — bred for disease resistance (coffee leaf rust), increasingly competitive cup quality
- **Character:** Clean, balanced, mild. Earlier generations were flat; newer selections (Castillo, Cenicafé 1) rival traditional varieties
- **Approach:** 92-94°C, 9 bar, classic profiles. Treat like a well-behaved Colombian
- **Risk:** Older hybrids (pre-2010 Castillo) can taste papery. Modern selections are much improved — don't prejudge

## Processing Method Patterns

### Washed (Wet Process)
- **Character:** Clean, bright, origin-forward
- **Extraction:** Straightforward, classic profiles work well
- **Pre-infusion:** Standard 4-6 seconds sufficient
- **Notes:** Most forgiving processing method

### Natural (Dry Process)
- **Character:** Fruity, wine-like, fermented notes
- **Extraction:** Benefits from bloom phase to control intensity
- **Pre-infusion:** Extended (6-10 seconds) + bloom
- **Notes:** Can be overwhelming without proper extraction control

### Honey (Pulped Natural)
- **Character:** Sweetness between washed and natural
- **Extraction:** Moderate pre-infusion (5-7 seconds)
- **Notes:** Yellow honey = cleaner, black honey = more fruit

### Anaerobic
- **Character:** Intense, funky, unique fermentation flavors
- **Extraction:** Careful temperature control, often lower
- **Pre-infusion:** Extended to manage intensity
- **Notes:** Can overwhelm if over-extracted

## Freshness Adjustment

### Days Off Roast Impact

| Days Off Roast | CO2 Level | Grind Adjustment |
|----------------|-----------|------------------|
| 3-7 days | High | 1-2 micro steps coarser |
| 8-14 days | Medium | Baseline (grind map reference) |
| 15-21 days | Low | 1-2 micro steps finer |
| 22+ days | Depleted | Consider fresher beans |

> *For comprehensive freshness science — degassing curves, peak flavor windows by roast level, storage methods, and freezing protocols — see `knowledge/BEAN_FRESHNESS_AND_STORAGE.md`.*

### Calculation Example

If grind-map.md shows a similar coffee at `9D` at 14 days off roast:
- New bag at 7 days → suggest `9F` or `9G` (coarser)
- New bag at 21 days → suggest `9B` or `9C` (finer)

## Photo Extraction Guide

When reading a coffee bag photo, look for:

1. **Roaster name** — Usually prominent branding
2. **Coffee name** — Often a farm, region, or creative name
3. **Origin** — Country, region, farm name
4. **Process** — May say "washed," "natural," "honey," etc.
5. **Roast date** — "Roasted on [date]" — critical for freshness
6. **Tasting notes** — Flavor descriptors
7. **Altitude** — Sometimes listed (higher = denser = finer grind)
8. **Variety** — Bourbon, Typica, Gesha, SL28, etc.

If any info is missing from the bag, search for it online.

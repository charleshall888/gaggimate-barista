The team settled on retiring the Sette 270 completely, adopting the DF64V with SSP Cast Lab Sweet V3 Red Speed as the sole grinder, and building the surrounding repo infrastructure as grinder-agnostic from the first keystroke rather than doing another Sette-to-DF64V hardcode that would need undoing later.

Three alternatives were weighed on the migration path. A combined grind map that preserved both Sette macro-micro codes and DF64V stepless marks in one table was dropped because the two notations have no defined numeric relationship and mixing them would mislead future lookups. Upfront profile re-engineering was set aside in favor of a phased approach — re-dial grind on the seasoned burr first, then nudge peak pressure and bloom duration only when a shot is diagnosed under-extracted (sour finish, fast pull on telemetry), not merely thin; thin body on a balanced shot is the expected flat-burr clarity trade and is not a trigger. Operating at the low RPM the Cast vendor recommends (~300 RPM for "surgical clarity") was discarded because that figure sits below the DF64V's stall floor and is a Zerno-grinder recommendation, not a DF64V one.

The concrete tradeoff accepted is patience during a fiddly dial-in. The V3 Red Speed is espresso-capable, but it lives at a fine working point with a possibly narrow choke margin, settings will drift coarser through roughly five to ten kilograms of seasoning before they stabilize, and the skills refactor that fully parameterizes the grinder layer is lifecycle-gated and cannot unblock the physical dial-in track.

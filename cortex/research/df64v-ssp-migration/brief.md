The research settled on a phased, gate-first migration: before any permanent repo work, pull a test shot inside the return window to confirm the DF64V with Cast V3 Red Speed burrs can actually build espresso pressure on light roasts, and only if that passes, proceed with archiving the Sette grind map, starting a fresh stepless map, and re-dialing grind on the seasoned burr before touching profiles at all.

Three things were weighed against that path. Up-front profile re-engineering was rejected because the burr drifts coarser through roughly five to ten kilograms of seasoning, so any early profile tweak just chases a moving target. A combined Sette-plus-DF64V grind map was rejected because the two notations share no defined relationship and mixing them would make future lookups actively misleading. Full grinder-plugin generalization — parameterizing the three skills and rewriting five shared knowledge files — was held as a deliberate second track rather than bundled into the personal migration, because it is net-new medium-effort work that exists only for the multi-user goal and can be deferred or dropped without blocking your own dial-in.

The accepted tradeoff is that the return-window gate is partly unreadable before seasoning, so an early "cannot choke" result is genuinely ambiguous — it could mean the rig is incapable of espresso pressure on light roasts, or it could mean the burrs are simply not yet seasoned. The mitigation is to run a short seasoning burst on stale beans and probe first with a medium roast, but if that still cannot reach resistance, the research is clear: return the burr rather than waiting on seasoning to rescue it.

# Burr Recommendation: SSP Cast Lab Sweet V3 Red Speed *(revised after critical review)*

**Honest framing up front:** This is a *ceiling/clarity* upgrade, not a problem-fix. You currently produce 5★ shots on the Sette 270 — six of them — and the active coffee is dialed in. The case for upgrading is "more separation on the coffees you care about," not "fix what isn't working." If "more clarity at the margin on coffees you're already enjoying" doesn't justify ~$684 in spend + 1-2 weeks of re-dialing, the right answer is to keep the Sette. The recommendation below assumes you've already decided to spend the money.

**Recommendation:** SSP Cast Lab Sweet V3 — Red Speed coated, 64mm, $185. *Best available evidence in early 2026, NOT settled science:* V3 has ~16 months on the market, no marquee long-form independent review yet (Hedrick's tier lists pre-date V3 release), and the load-bearing taste descriptors come from retailers that sell the burr.

**Context:** Baratza Sette 270 + Gaggia Classic Pro + Gaggimate Pro (pressure profiling, bloom-slide at 7.5 bar peak typical). 22g IMS Baristapro Nanotech basket. Single-dose workflow (WDT → tamp, no leveler). Primarily small milk drinks (cortado, cappuccino, flat white). Active coffee is Choco Coffee Hacienda La Papaya Typica Anaerobic (Light-Medium, 120hr anaerobic, Ecuador Loja, raspberry/cocoa/stone fruit). Budget: $800 for grinder + burrs (this allocation has not itself been compared against non-grinder spends — see "Scope assumptions" below).

## Reasoning (revised, with epistemic flags)

1. **Your rotation skews to V3's target band** — but read this carefully. 67% of your rated shots are Light/Light-Medium and 75% are Natural/Anaerobic; all six 5★ shots fall in that intersection. **This matches the input distribution exactly**, so the 6/6 result is consistent with zero processing-method affinity — it's bean-style preference + brewing volume, not necessarily evidence that anaerobic-naturals respond *better* to a clarity-leaning burr than washed coffees would on the same rig. The honest claim is: V3 is well-matched to the coffees you choose to buy. The stronger claim (V3 maximizes *intrinsic* fit) is unproven from your shot log.

2. **MP V2 vs V3 — the body argument is weaker than originally stated.** The strongest milk-drink format you pull is cortado (1:1 espresso:milk, the *least* milk-heavy format on your list); cappuccino and flat white are heavier on milk. MP V2 reviewers do describe the burr as thin and unforgiving, but "needs body for cortado" is a soft argument. **Your own catalogued tasting vocabulary** (`vivid`, `raspberry vivid`, `precision over jammy chaos`, `Typica's characteristic clarity`) is MP V2's home territory. If you decide milk-drink body matters less than fruit clarity, MP V2 Red Speed is the legitimate alternative pick — it's the "fruit notes pop the loudest" choice in this lineup.

3. **HU is too body-heavy** for the raspberry/cocoa/stone-fruit signature; would push toward dark milk chocolate.

4. **V3 over V2:** the V3 rotor has more cutting edges and was reportedly designed for espresso gap/pressure. *Source caveat:* "~15% more cutting edges" and "designed for espresso" come from the Zerno V3 launch announcement, i.e. the seller. V2's "fiddly espresso dial-in" reputation is not pinned to a specific reviewer in my sources — treat as conventional-wisdom claim, not measured fact.

5. **Red Speed over Silver Knight:** $65 cheaper AND the extra controlled fines support body for milk drinks.

6. **DF64V variable-RPM premium is barely used by the recommended operating plan.** Run V3 at 1100-1200 RPM (just above the load-trip floor). This is a ~100 RPM band — effectively a fixed-speed grinder. A **DF64 Gen 2** (fixed ~1400 RPM, ~$100-150 cheaper) lands at a similar working point and accepts the same burr swap. If the body decision is still open, Gen 2 is a defensible cost-saving move that doesn't compromise the burr pick. (Variable RPM matters more if you also want filter or significantly slower-RPM body work — neither is in your current rotation.)

## Cost-of-change (newly accounted)

- **Profile re-dialing:** every per-coffee profile in `coffees/*/` was authored against the Sette's high-fines bimodal output at 7.5 bar bloom-slide peak. A unimodal flat will likely want finer grind and higher peak pressure (closer to 9 bar per `EXTRACTION_SCIENCE.md`). Plan on 1-2 weeks of re-dialing your active rotation.
- **`grind-map.md` vocabulary becomes a historical artifact** — 13E/13F macro+micro letters don't translate to a stepless collar. You'll be re-learning the dialing grammar.
- **Workflow change:** Sette is chute-direct-to-portafilter; DF64V is bellows + catch cup → WDT → tamp. Single-dose in both cases but the mechanics differ.
- **Specific post-purchase risks to watch** (under-investigated because 4 Home-Barista threads returned HTTP 403): burr alignment shims may be needed out-of-box, retention behavior on a new SSP set, batch variation on small-run releases, dial-in jumps near choke. Worth budgeting a return-window check.
